# Cardboard 6-DOF Robotic Arm

## Overview

This project focuses on the design and development of a low-cost
6-degree-of-freedom robotic arm prototype using cardboard as the
primary structural material.

The project demonstrates the fundamental concepts of robotic arm
mechanisms, joint motion, link arrangement, and low-cost mechanical
prototyping.

## Objectives

- Design a 6-degree-of-freedom robotic arm.
- Develop a functional low-cost mechanical prototype.
- Understand the arrangement and motion of robotic joints.
- Explore robotic arm link and joint mechanisms.
- Demonstrate the working principle of a multi-degree-of-freedom
  manipulator.
- Develop a lightweight prototype using readily available materials.

## System Description

The robotic arm consists of multiple interconnected links and joints
that provide six degrees of freedom.

The cardboard structure was used to construct the robotic arm links
and supporting mechanical components, allowing the mechanism to be
developed as an inexpensive prototype.

The six degrees of freedom allow the arm to achieve different
orientations and positions within its workspace.

## Mechanical Design

The arm was designed as a lightweight cardboard prototype to study
the mechanical arrangement of a multi-joint robotic manipulator.

The design consists of:

- Base
- Multiple robotic arm links
- Rotational joints
- End-effector mechanism
- Structural supports

The joints allow relative movement between the links, enabling the
end effector to reach different positions and orientations.

## Degrees of Freedom

The robotic arm incorporates six independent degrees of freedom.

These degrees of freedom provide multiple axes of movement and allow
the manipulator to perform positioning and orientation tasks.

## Prototype Development

Cardboard was selected as the primary construction material because
it is lightweight, inexpensive, and easy to cut and assemble.

The prototype development involved:

1. Designing the individual arm links.
2. Preparing the cardboard components.
3. Assembling the links and joints.
4. Integrating the end-effector mechanism.
5. Testing the movement of the assembled arm.
6. Evaluating the mechanical structure and range of motion.
